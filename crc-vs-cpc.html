CRC vs CPC: Which Certification Is Right for You?

Both are respected AAPC coding credentials, but they prepare you for different types of work. Here is a clear comparison of what each certification covers, who each is best for, and how to decide which path fits your goals.

The biggest difference between CRC and CPC

The CPC is a broad professional coding credential focused on physician and outpatient services. AAPC says the CPC validates knowledge of CPT®, HCPCS Level II, and ICD-10-CM, along with modifiers, coding guidelines, compliance, payer rules, and reimbursement concepts.

The CRC is a more specialized credential focused on risk adjustment and diagnosis coding. The CRC exam concentrates heavily on ICD-10-CM, risk adjustment models, documentation improvement, compliance, HCC-related concepts, and coding diagnoses from medical records.

Simple way to think about it: If you want broad physician/outpatient coding skills, CPC may be the better first fit. If your goal is specifically risk adjustment, HCC coding, chronic-condition documentation, and diagnosis-focused coding, CRC may be the more direct path.

CPC

What does the CPC cover?

AAPC describes the CPC as a broad credential for professional coding across physician and outpatient services. The CPC tests your ability to apply CPT, HCPCS Level II, and ICD-10-CM codes and understand topics such as modifiers, E/M services, coding guidelines, medical necessity, payer requirements, compliance, and reimbursement.

Because it spans multiple code sets and many types of professional services, the CPC can be a strong choice for someone who wants a broad coding foundation or is not yet certain which coding specialty they want to pursue.

The CPC may be a good fit if you:

  • Want broad exposure to medical coding
  • Want to learn procedure coding as well as diagnosis coding
  • Are interested in physician-office or outpatient professional coding
  • Want a credential that can serve as a foundation for later specialties
  • Are still exploring which coding area you like best

CRC

What does the CRC cover?

The CRC is focused on the knowledge used in risk adjustment coding. AAPC’s current CRC exam blueprint includes 30 diagnosis-coding questions, 15 compliance questions, 12 documentation-improvement questions, 15 risk-adjustment-model questions, and additional questions on the purpose and use of risk adjustment models, quality of care, pathophysiology, terminology, anatomy, and medical-record cases.

The exam relies heavily on ICD-10-CM diagnosis coding and the ability to determine which documented conditions are accurately reportable in a risk adjustment context.

The CRC may be a good fit if you:

  • Want to work specifically in risk adjustment or HCC coding
  • Enjoy ICD-10-CM and diagnosis coding
  • Are interested in chronic-condition documentation
  • Want to work with medical-record review, compliance, audits, or provider documentation
  • Already work in healthcare and want to transition into risk adjustment

Prerequisites

Do you need the CPC before the CRC?

No. AAPC currently lists the CRC as a stand-alone certification. You do not have to earn a CPC or COC first before taking the CRC exam.

AAPC also states that there is no required amount of specialty experience to register for the CRC exam, although approximately two years of relevant experience is highly recommended because it is considered a difficult, high-level exam.

Both CPC and CRC candidates must meet AAPC’s basic exam-registration requirements, including current AAPC membership, a valid exam voucher, current contact information, and valid government-issued photo identification.

Remember: AAPC exam eligibility and employer hiring requirements are two different things. A company may require experience, education, or multiple credentials even when AAPC does not require those items just to sit for an exam.

Long-term growth

Should you eventually earn both CPC and CRC?

You can. The two credentials complement each other because they demonstrate different skill sets.

A coder who holds both CPC and CRC may have a broad professional-coding foundation along with specialized risk adjustment knowledge. That can be useful for professionals whose work crosses physician coding, diagnosis coding, risk adjustment, auditing, compliance, or provider education.

But you do not need to earn both simply because both exist. Start with the credential that best matches the work you actually want to do.

Frequently asked questions

CRC vs CPC FAQ

Is the CRC easier than the CPC?

They test different skill sets, so “easier” depends on your background. Someone strong in diagnosis coding and risk adjustment may find CRC content more familiar, while someone experienced in physician coding may be more comfortable with CPC content.

Can I get the CRC without a CPC?

Yes. AAPC lists the CRC as a stand-alone certification.

Which credential uses CPT?

The CPC heavily tests CPT procedure coding. The CRC is primarily diagnosis- and risk-adjustment-focused and uses ICD-10-CM as its approved code-book reference for the exam.

Which certification is better for risk adjustment?

If your goal is specifically risk adjustment and HCC coding, the CRC is the credential designed for that specialty.

Which certification is better for a beginner?

That depends on the beginner’s goal. CPC provides a broader professional-coding foundation, while CRC provides a more direct route for someone committed to risk adjustment. AAPC advises beginners without healthcare experience to build medical terminology, anatomy, and pathophysiology knowledge before certification training.
