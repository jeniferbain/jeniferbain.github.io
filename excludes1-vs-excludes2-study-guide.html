Excludes1 vs Excludes2

ICD-10-CM visual study guide

Why this matters

Excludes notes appear in the Tabular List and help determine whether conditions may be reported together. Always read the complete note before assigning codes.

⛔ Excludes1

Generally not coded together

  • Means NOT CODED HERE. The excluded condition should not normally be reported with the code above the note.
  • Codes typically describe mutually exclusive conditions, such as two forms of the same condition.
  • Exception: If the two conditions are unrelated, both codes may be reported when otherwise appropriate. If the relationship is unclear, query the provider.
  • Always check the note in the Tabular List.

✓ Excludes2

Both codes may be used when appropriate

  • Means NOT INCLUDED HERE.
  • The excluded condition is not part of the condition represented by the code.
  • A patient may have both conditions at the same time.
  • If both conditions are documented and meet reporting rules, both codes may be assigned.

What to do when you see an Excludes note

1
Read the entire note at the code, subcategory or category.
2
Determine what conditions are documented.
3
Identify Excludes1 or Excludes2 and whether an exception applies.
4
Verify reporting, specificity, sequencing and other instructions.

Original study examples

Excludes1 — Actual FY 2027 guideline exampleF45.8 (Other somatoform disorders) has an Excludes1 note for G47.63 (Sleep-related teeth grinding). You should not assign both codes for the same teeth-grinding condition. However, when a patient has psychogenic dysmenorrhea (classified to F45.8) and separately has sleep-related teeth grinding (G47.63), both codes may be reported because those conditions are unrelated.
Excludes2 — How to apply the ruleSuppose the Tabular List has an Excludes2 note for a separate condition not included in the first code. If both conditions are documented and both are reportable, you may assign both codes. This note is not a requirement to code both when only one condition is present.

CPC exam reminders

✓ Never stop at the Alphabetic Index.   ✓ Verify notes in the Tabular List.   ✓ Do not assume two diagnoses may or may not be coded together without considering the Excludes note and documentation.
