Current CPC exam format

Know the target before you review

AAPC's current CPC exam assesses 17 areas of knowledge. Most questions use coding scenarios involving CPT®, HCPCS Level II, and ICD-10-CM, and the exam ends with 10 medical-record cases. You have four hours for 100 multiple-choice questions.

CPC study checklist

What should you review before the CPC exam?

A final CPC review should reinforce the major exam domains while helping you find information quickly and apply coding rules accurately.

CPT® coding

Review E/M, anesthesia, surgery by body system, radiology, pathology/laboratory, and medicine. Pay attention to section guidelines, parenthetical instructions, add-on codes, and code relationships.

ICD-10-CM

Practice using the Alphabetic Index and Tabular List together. Reinforce instructional notes, sequencing, combination codes, laterality, manifestations, and chapter-specific guidelines.

HCPCS Level II

Review supplies, medications, durable medical equipment, and other services represented by HCPCS Level II codes, along with relevant conventions and modifiers.

Modifiers

Make sure you understand when commonly tested CPT® and HCPCS modifiers change how a service is reported and when a modifier is not appropriate.

Anatomy & medical terminology

Refresh terminology and anatomy across body systems. These concepts help you interpret documentation and are also tested directly on the CPC exam.

Compliance & regulatory concepts

Review the compliance and regulatory topics included in the CPC blueprint, including Medicare concepts, NCCI edits, NCDs/LCDs, HIPAA, ABNs, fraud and abuse, place of service, and RVUs.

Medical-record cases

Practice reading complete documentation and determining the diagnoses and procedures that are supported. The final portion of the current exam contains 10 cases.

Code-book navigation

Know where guidelines, tables, appendices, symbols, and frequently used sections are located. Your approved books are references—not a substitute for knowing how to navigate them quickly.

Final preparation

What to do in the weeks before your exam

Practice with a timer

Four hours for 100 questions works out to an average of about 2.4 minutes per question. Practice moving on when a question is consuming too much time and returning to difficult questions later.

  • Complete timed question sets
  • Practice all major code-set sections
  • Review rationales after each set
  • Track recurring weak areas

Prepare your books correctly

AAPC currently allows the AMA CPT® Professional Edition plus one ICD-10-CM and one HCPCS Level II book. Current-year books are highly recommended; the immediately preceding year's books are also allowed under the current policy.

  • Know AAPC's current note rules
  • Use tabs only within current policy
  • Do not insert prohibited study-guide or exam questions
  • Recheck AAPC rules shortly before test day

Frequently asked questions

CPC Exam Review FAQ

What should I study most for the CPC exam?

Review all exam domains, but use practice questions to identify where you personally need the most work. Be comfortable with CPT®, ICD-10-CM, HCPCS Level II, modifiers, terminology, anatomy, compliance concepts, medical-record cases, and code-book navigation.

How many questions are on the CPC exam?

The current CPC exam has 100 multiple-choice questions and a four-hour time limit.

What score do I need to pass?

AAPC currently requires an overall score of 70% or higher, meaning at least 70 correct answers out of 100.

Should I take CPC practice exams?

Practice exams can help you work on timing, identify weak areas, and become more comfortable applying coding concepts. Review the rationales rather than focusing only on your score.

Is this CPC Exam Review for complete beginners?

No. The exam review is intended for students who have already completed coding education and are preparing for the certification exam. Beginners who still need to learn coding should start with a full CPC training program.
